# FMPS code review, 2026-10-18

A review of the Matlab/Octave scripts in `fmps/` and `examples/` (plus the
Fortran they call, where needed), looking for serious bugs and
inconsistencies. It was done to support the Julia port. No repository files
were changed by the review itself.

## Rating

**Overall: about 7/10.** The core FMPS solve is trustworthy, as both this
review and the Julia port found. The documentation, examples and tests
around it are research code that was never cleaned up for others. Most
problems are convention, sign and orientation issues at the reporting
boundary, not in the solve.

| Area | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Core solver physics and numerics | 9 | Matches Mie to all digits; ext = sca + abs to 1e-11; the three tiers agree; dielectric host correct; 7e-15 against an independent solver. Points off for the unreliable FMM top order and the Bessel overflow at extreme ε (items 12, 13). |
| Conventions and documentation | 5 | Self-consistent, but its own conventions are undocumented (A/B order, impedance-normalized H, Euler angles). Some docstrings are wrong (ra/rb sign, array lengths, the `fmps_solver` rotation comment). The `framerot.f` handedness slip comes from this (items 1, 9, 10). |
| Examples and post-processing | 5 | The main drivers run and agree with the solver, but there are the half-size Mie cross sections, mislabeled units and a coarse Poynting grid in `fmps_scan`, the incomplete `fmps_b_post_far`, the `pvec` sign, and stale `fmps_extra_tests` (items 3–8). |
| Tests | 4 | All 13 run with small errors, but nothing asserts. `test7` computes transmission coefficients and never uses them. No test covers a generic type-3 orientation. |
| Robustness and maintenance | 5 | Crashes from missing functions and MEX stubs, `toc` without `tic`, unchecked GMRES convergence, and an `e3fgrid` stub relying on the Fortran writing into an input-only array. No macOS binary, and builds rewrite tracked files. |

A modest cleanup, the to-do list below, would raise it to about 8.5–9.

## Serious: wrong results

The solver is self-consistent: expansions are formed, reflected and
translated with one convention throughout. The physics checks confirm it
(single sphere vs. Mie, ext = sca + abs, agreement with the Julia T-matrices).
The sign problems in items 1 and 3 sit where results are reported, not inside
the solve, which is why they did not change the physical picture in the
shipped examples. The ra/rb sign (item 9) and the A/B order are documentation
and convention only. Items 4 and 5 change the numbers those scripts print,
not the solver's answer.

1. **Type-3 orientation uses the opposite β sign from the documented and
   plotted convention.** *run*
   - `em3d_multa_r` (type-3 branch) applies `emabrotaf`, the matrix, then
     `emabrotab`. That orients the particle as Rz(−α)Ry(−β)Rz(−γ).
   - `em3orient` / `framerotf`, the `rotangles` docstrings and
     `plot_inclusion(s)` use Rz(−α)Ry(+β)Rz(−γ).
   - Author's note: the multipole rotations were meant to follow the
     fmmlib3d conventions, and they do. *read*
     - `emabrotaf`/`emabrotab` are built from fmmlib3d's pieces: the
       e^{imφ} phase (`mpolerotz`) and `rotviarecur3` about y
       (`mpoleroty`).
     - `src/fmps/rotviarecur3.f` is identical to fmmlib3d's apart from the
       license header.
     - The composition matches `h3dmpmp` in `fmmlib3d/src/h3dtrans.f:290-355`.
     - All three steps turn the same way, so Rz(−α)Ry(−β)Rz(−γ) is a
       proper ZYZ Euler rotation.
   - The inconsistency is a handedness slip inside `src/fmps/framerot.f`,
     not a difference from fmmlib3d. *read*
     - `framepntr(x,y,α)` rotates clockwise:
       (x,y) → (x cosα + y sinα, −x sinα + y cosα).
     - `framerotz` applies it to (x,y), giving Rz(−α).
     - `frameroty` applies it to (x,z). A right-handed y rotation needs
       the order (z,x), so this gives Ry(+α). `pointroty` has the same
       slip.
     - So `framerotf` is not a ZYZ Euler rotation in either the active or
       the passive sense.
   - Test: a random non-symmetric T and the real `scat.freq.70` T,
     compared with the rotated incident field and back-rotated scattered
     field. With R = `em3orient(rot)` the error is O(1) for β ≠ π/2. With
     R = `em3orient([α,−β,γ])` the error is 1e-15 (file T) and 5e-14
     (random T).
   - The comment at `fmps_solver.m:56-58` is wrong as well: (0, π/2, 0) maps
     z → −x and x → z.
   - The β = π/2 presets differ only by an axis sign flip, which a
     mirror-symmetric particle hides (the two-ellipsoid file agrees to 1e-9).
     Uniform orientation averages are unaffected. Any fixed generic
     orientation is affected, for example aligned rods, or the 2010
     x-oriented ellipsoid pair if it is used at generic angles.
   - The comments in `src/fmps/emabrot3var.f` are swapped: `emabrotab` says
     "forward", `emabrotaf` says "backward".
   - Fix: in `frameroty` and `pointroty`, call
     `framepntr(vert(3),vert(1),alpha)`. Then correct the `rotangles`
     docstrings and the `fmps_solver.m:56-58` comment (the β = π/2 presets
     then map z → −x), and repeat the deterministic test.
     - This leaves solver results unchanged and keeps the multipole
       rotations consistent with fmmlib3d.
     - The alternative, negating β in `em3d_multa_r` or `emabrot*`, would
       move away from fmmlib3d.
   - Effect on results: the solve is self-consistent and the computed
     particle is a valid rotated particle, just not the one described.
     - Unaffected: spheres (types 1 and 2); uniform orientation averages,
       because flipping β maps a uniform set of orientations onto another
       uniform set; and the mirror-symmetric two-ellipsoid particle at the
       β = π/2 presets.
     - Affected: a type-3 particle at a generic fixed orientation. For
       β = π/4 the long axis is 90° from the plotted one. No shipped
       example exercises this case, which is why the slip went unnoticed.
   - Confirmed against a converged full-wave solve by the Julia port: the
     solver's `A.rot` is the body→lab map R = Rz(−α)Ry(−β)Rz(−γ), in
     standard counter-clockwise matrices. All 7 other candidate
     conventions, including the β-flipped one, are off by 8% to 8×. *run*
2. **`rotangles(6)` ("random orientation") is not uniform on SO(3).**
   `fmps/rotangles.m:77` draws β = π·rand. A uniform draw is
   β = acos(1−2·rand). Averages built on it oversample the poles, so they
   are biased. (The Julia port uses its own uniform draw and is unaffected.)
   *read*
3. **`empol`/`empolms` return the electric dipole p with the wrong sign;
   m is correct.** *run*
   - Where: `src/fmps/fmpsrouts.f:53-90`; called from `examples/fmps_scan.m:314`.
   - PEC sphere a=5 at λ=5000: `pvec` = −125 x̂, while the scattered E on the
     x axis gives p = +a³E0 = +125 x̂.
   - The magnetic moment is correct, m = +62.5 ŷ = −(a³/2)H0. Here
     H0 = −ŷ because the drivers use `kvec = zk*[0 0 -1]`.
   - A dielectric quasi-static sphere confirms it: the near field gives +α,
     `pvec` gives −α.
   - `examples/fmps-3.0-mi/FMPS_post_mom.m` negates both moments, which
     makes m wrong.
   - Author's note: FMPS uses its own nonstandard convention for the A and B
     multipoles and the electric and magnetic dipoles. Eventually it should
     be cleaned up to be consistent with Debye's conventions and others in
     the literature.
   - The convention does not account for this sign. *read, run*
     - `empol` applies the same formula to `bmpole` to get p and to
       `ampole` to get m.
     - Under E↔H duality the TM and TE fields differ by a sign, so p and m
       cannot both come out with the physical sign.
     - Any single convention would treat both moments alike. Here they
       disagree with each other, so this is a bug within the current
       convention.
   - Effect on results: `empol` is post-processing only.
     - Unaffected: the solution, cross sections, fields, far fields and |p|.
     - Affected: the real and imaginary parts of p plotted at
       `fmps_scan.m:344` (mirrored), and the relative phase of p and m,
       which matters when the two are combined, as in directional
       (Kerker-type) scattering or a dipole far field built from p and m.
     - `fmps-3.0-mi/FMPS_post_mom.m:57-58` negates both moments before the
       SI conversion. That was probably done because p came out with the
       wrong sign against expected gold-rod polarizabilities. It fixes p
       and flips m, which is small for gold rods and easy to miss.
   - Fix: negate only the three `pvec` lines. During the Debye cleanup, a
     Rayleigh-sphere test pins both signs: p = αE0, and for PEC

## Inconsistencies and traps for the Julia port

9. **Sign in the ra/rb docstrings.** `rcoefs_die_arb*.m:24-25` and the
   coated variants say ra and rb "are the b and a of ... Bohren &
   Huffman". Numerically, ra = −b_BH and rb = −a_BH (to 2e-16). The
   docstrings also say `complex(nterms+1)`, but the arrays have length
   nterms (n = 1..nterms). The wording came from commit 778a914; the peer
   session (its author) agreed it should be corrected. *run*
   - The swapped A/B order itself is an FMPS convention, not a bug.
     `ampole` is TE (r·E = 0, the magnetic multipole) and `bmpole` is TM
     (the electric multipole). Bohren & Huffman and the Debye-potential
     literature list the electric/TM coefficient first as a_n. See the
     author's note under item 3.
10. **H is impedance-normalized and undocumented.** Throughout the code,
    H = curl E/(ik) = Z_host·H_phys. `em3d_planearb_targeval` builds H = k̂×E,
    and `em3ehformta` needs this convention (feeding it the physical H
    changes a and b by O(1)). Multiply output H by sqrt(eps0/mu0) for the
    physical H. Not a bug; document it in `em3d_planearb_targeval`,
    `em3ehformta` and `em3d_mpole_targeval`. *run*
11. **`rmatr_file` performs no checks.** It discards the file's omega, rk
    and radius without comparing them with `A`, and it ignores the
    per-column (n,m) labels. The assumed n-major order is correct. All
    type-3 spheres share one matrix. *run*
12. **Bessel overflow.** `emjevalrt`/`emhevalrt` fail for |Im z| above about
    1e3. eps = −1e6 gives NaN in `rcoefs_die_arb`, and eps = 1e8·i returns
    garbage. Use scaled Bessel ratios, or switch to `rcoefs_pec`. *run*
13. **FMM tier (`em3d_multa_mptafmm`).** *run*
    - The unused |m| > n output slots contain uninitialized memory, which
      varies from run to run and with the thread count. `em3dzero`
      (`src/fmps/emfmm3drouts_e2.f:775`) zeroes only |m| ≤ n, and
      `em3dmpolesort` copies the whole uninitialized workspace back.
    - Harmless in the solver, because `em3sphlin` drops those slots. When
      comparing raw arrays, mask |m| ≤ n, n ≥ 1.
    - The top order n = nterms is unreliable (relative error 27 at nterms 13
      and 208 at nterms 18, against about 0.14 for the f90 tier). Lower
      orders are more accurate than the f90 tier. The cause is suspected:
      box expansions and grids sized by `nterms(level)` rather than nterms0.
    - Full-solve field errors stay around 1e-5 to 1e-7. The top-order error
      matters for particles with a strong top-order response.
14. **`em3tata3_trunc` is wrong when `nterms1 > nterms`** (it differs from
    `em3tata3` by 1e4 to 1e11; out-of-bounds suspected). It is fine
    in the large-to-small use in `fmps_b.m`. *run*
15. **MEX interface (`.mw`).** Every `# FORTRAN` line matches its subroutine
    in argument count, order and type. *run*
    - `e3fgrid` declares `rnodes` as input-only (`i double[]`), but the
      Fortran writes into it in place. This breaks copy-on-write; declare it
      `inout`. A mutation test found no other wrapper that writes in place.
    - `em3ehformta`/`em3ehformmp` pass `double[1] sphere_r` where the
      Fortran expects `center(3)`. Harmless only because the Fortran never
      reads `center`.
16. **Iterative solvers.**
    - `examples/iterate/bicgstab_simple.m:66,95,97` uses β = sqrt(ρ_k/ρ_{k−1})·α/ω
      instead of (ρ_k/ρ_{k−1})·α/ω. It still converges, with about twice the
      iterations. The `n==1` branch crashes, and the loop runs only maxit−1
      iterations. *run*
    - The GMRES routines are correct: they match Octave's `gmres` and
      `A\b` to 1e-10. *run*
    - No driver checks the GMRES `ier`, and all run at tol 1e-3 with 20–40
      iterations, so non-convergence passes silently. *read*
    - Comparison with the upstream library `~/gitlab/matlab/iterate`:
      `gmres_simple`, `gmres_restart` and `gmres_cycle` are byte-identical
      to it. `bicgstab_simple.m` is out of date: upstream already fixes β,
      the `n==1` case and the loop start, and adds breakdown checks

## Application check: magnetic response of the cut-wire pair

The pair of gold ellipsoids (rods along z, centers 40 nm apart along x,
normal y) is the cut-wire-pair meta-atom the project used to model negative
μ. This review computed its dipole polarizabilities from the type-3 files and
estimated ε_eff and μ_eff for aligned and random arrays. *run*

- **Method.**
  - Keep only the n = 1 TE (or TM) part of the incident expansion, so that
    m = α_m H(0) and p = α_e E(0) leave out electric-quadrupole excitation.
  - Read the moments from `empol`, with `pvec` negated (item 3).
  - Apply Clausius–Mossotti, (x−1)/(x+2) = (4π/3)Nα, with the radiation
    reaction removed for a lattice.
  - Validation: on spheres the method gives α_e = a³ and α_m = −a³/2 for
    PEC exactly, and matches Mie for a gold-like sphere to all printed
    digits.
- **Polarizabilities.**
  - Electric peak (Im α_e,zz) at 585 nm; magnetic peak (Im α_m,yy) at
    614 nm, with α_m = 511 + 3928i nm³.
  - |α_m/α_e| is about 2–3%.
  - Against the converged Julia T-matrices: α_e agrees to 0.3–2%. α_m
    agrees to 0.3–1.4% at 521–689 nm, but only 4.5–8% at 954–1016 nm,
    where the 2010 files are non-passive. Do not trust α_m from the 2010
    files above about 765 nm.
- **Effective medium** (vacuum host, cubic lattice):
  - Lattice spacing 100 nm (4.9% gold fill, the densest FMPS can model),
    pairs aligned:
    - Re ε along the rods dips to −0.15 at 585 nm, negative only at
      574–590 nm, with Im ε ≈ 1.9.
    - Re μ stays between 0.982 and 1.031.
  - At 156 nm spacing: Re ε 0.82–1.51, Re μ 0.995–1.008.
- **Conclusion.** This pair cannot give μ < 0. That would need roughly one
  pair per 25 nm cube, smaller than the pair itself. A denser film would
  push ε well negative (anisotropic, possibly hyperbolic), but μ would
  barely move. Optical magnetism needs a stronger meta-atom: longer or
  thicker rods, a smaller gap, or a resonance further into the infrared.
- **Caveats.**
  - Vacuum host; a real host (n ≈ 1.5) shifts both resonances red.
  - Dipole-only homogenization: the antisymmetric mode carries an electric
    quadrupole of the same order as m.
  - No lattice-sum corrections; Clausius–Mossotti is unreliable at
    densities beyond FMPS's range.

  `run_pair.m`, `check_sphere.m` and `xcheck.m` in
  `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-gimbutas-linux-repositories-fmps/0ffed302-2d27-4617-9873-00d27d94b9b2/scratchpad/pair-mu/`
